# NIST AI 600-1 -- Generative AI Profile: One-Pager

**Framework:** NIST AI 600-1, Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework: Generative Artificial Intelligence Profile (July 2024; NIST Editorial Review Board approval 25 July 2024; DOI 10.6028/NIST.AI.600-1; 64 pp)

**Status:** Current, unrevised since publication. ## Headline verdict

**Equips a decision-maker it never obligates, names, or waits for.**

This is the only NIST-executed instance of the core's Section 6 profile slot, and the slot is filled in form and empty in function. Cross-sectoral by design, the profile cannot exercise a user's risk tolerance, so the one property that makes a profile a profile under the core's own definition is blank by construction, and the tolerance-exercising instance the core actually describes remains unexecuted by anyone, two years on. The transmission test closes on all four properties by count rather than assertion: profiling the method framework transmitted no floor, no trigger, no obligating voice, and no acceptor. What profiling did transmit is richer decision furniture -- go/no-go vocabulary, a halt-plan row, one named escalation role, a drill imperative, an incident-report schema. Craft without force. The verified zero matters because the ecosystem prices this document as if it were not zero: procurement clauses, crosswalks and statutory references trade "aligned with 600-1" as attestation, an assurance the text cannot support.

## Scope

Cross-sectoral profile for generative AI, self-defined through the core's Section 6 mechanism and commissioned by EO 14110 s.4.1(a)(i)(A). Twelve enumerated risks, alphabetical and unranked, admitted only where an empirical evidence base existed at writing; speculative risks of more advanced future systems are excluded by the document's own scoping sentence, and the catalogue grows only "as the space evolves and empirical evidence indicates additional risks". Coverage follows the consultation's charter, not a risk analysis: the GAI Public Working Group's focus was "limited to" four considerations (governance, content provenance, pre-deployment testing, incident disclosure) and the suggested actions "primarily address these considerations"; footnote 13 concedes the partial subcategory coverage without inventorying the gaps. Forty-nine of the core's subcategories carry tables; 213 suggested actions in total (212 well-formed Action IDs plus GV4.3--001, malformed in the print itself, page-image-verified). MANAGE 1.4, the core subcategory whose subject is residual-risk documentation, is among those left without rows.

## Risk approach

The enumeration reversal: the core refuses to name risks; the profile names twelve and files them alphabetically. The evidence filter does the governing. Risks enter on demonstration, not anticipation, so the catalogue runs behind the evidence by design -- the opposite arrow from the frontier frameworks, which pre-commit responses ahead of it. On the shared hazard the difference is now demonstrated rather than inferred: CBRN (2.1) names a real mechanism and holds it at arm's length -- the hazard in the future conditional, the uplift evidence accurately reported as not-yet against studies current at publication, and nothing in the entry wired to the day that changes -- while in the interval since, the labs' triggered machinery moved as their evidence moved (a live High designation in Biological and Chemical on a deployed model family; ASL-3 CBRN protections maintained against named benchmarks) and this unsponsored text still reports 2024. On enforceability all four documents are self-judged; the meaningful difference is the direction of the arrow. One register note: the entry's most forward-leaning material (biological design tools, capabilities the entry concedes "lie beyond the reach of existing GAI tools") sits inside a document whose own filter forbids speculation; the commissioning order defined the document's subject with CBRN barrier-lowering as its first danger criterion, a fingerprint reading carried as labelled interpretation in the reasoning note.

## Enforcement model

None. Inherited from the parent by citation and confirmed at profile scale by census, word-boundary across the whole text: shall, zero; mandatory, zero; operative must, zero (the document's sole "must" is a cited paper title asserting that calibrated language models must hallucinate); should, seven, none creating an obligation internal to the document, the firmest deferring to human-subjects research regimes; require-stem, nineteen, every occurrence descriptive, imported header text, or deference to external law. Against that: may 99, can 123, could 25. All 213 suggested actions are imperative to the reader; adoption is nowhere recorded; nobody can be found in breach of any row. Residual risk appears exactly once, passive, inside an imported header; risk acceptance exactly once, as an unattributed item in an imported header's treatment menu; no acceptor exists anywhere. On the corpus's residual-risk spectrum the profile sits precisely where its parent sits: none.

## Who it binds

No one, and it never claims otherwise. Every decision verb belongs to the reader: the organisation aligns, decides, prioritises, tailors; risk tolerance is invoked as an assumed input at least six times (GOVERN 1.3 header; MS-2.6 header and MS-2.6-003; MG-2.4-004; MG-3.1-001; MG-3.2-009) and set nowhere. The only binding edge the document ever carried pointed at NIST itself -- the EO's direction to write it -- and that obligation was discharged at publication and revoked in January 2025. The text still opens by citing the dead order and defines its own subject matter, generative AI and the dual-use foundation model, in the dead order's words (footnotes 1 and 2).

## Key obligations

*Field kept per the parent's pattern; the obligating voice it assumes does not exist in this document. The entries below are the document's strongest instructions. None obligates.*

- Devise a plan to halt development or deployment of a GAI system posing unacceptable negative risk (GV-1.3-007); establish minimum go/no-go thresholds for deployment approval (GV-1.3-002). The decision machinery the frontier labs run, present here as instructions to build it.
- Establish third-party incident response with defined ownership, rehearsal at a cadence, and retrospective learning (GV-6.2-003); practice and follow incident response plans (MG-4.2-002).
- Re-evaluate safety features when negative risk exceeds organisational risk tolerance (MS-2.6-003): the only threshold-conditional in 213 rows, and it fires a re-evaluation, against a tolerance the document assumes exists.
- Escalate incidents to the organisational risk management authority when deactivation criteria are met (MG-2.4-002); establish and review those criteria "in accordance with set risk tolerances and appetites" (MG-2.4-004).
- Establish an incident-report field schema -- system, reporter, date, impacts (GV-4.3-002) -- for reports the document elsewhere concedes have nowhere formal to go.

Assembled in sequence, these rows form an escalation chain; the document never assembles it. A parts catalogue for a decision system, with no assembled system.

## Intersections with crisis / resilience

- Judged as raw material, several tables are the strongest government-published GAI resilience content to date: rollover and fallback including reversion to manual processing (GV-6.2-006), data redundancy down to model weights (GV-6.2-005), rehearsal and drill imperatives, the incident-report schema. The craft does not move the answer; the answer does not diminish the craft.
- A.1.8, the document's only connected incident prose, fails sign-off as doctrine. Its definition is borrowed (OECD, May 2024), hedged as "can be defined as", and broadened twice in the borrowing: the causal bar lowered from leads-to to contributes-to, and psychological harm added. It is imported without its companion term: "hazard" appears zero times in the document, so the near-miss tier -- the leading-indicator layer mature incident doctrine runs on -- does not exist here. Red pen: no owner, no trigger, no clock, no recipient, with the recipient absence confessed in the section's own most concrete sentence: "Formal channels do not currently exist to report and document AI incidents." Compel-delta against ISO 22301 or NIMS-class doctrine: zero. Residual value: an accurate map of an empty field, and one sharp observation that incident attribution will break through distributed third-party plugins with inconsistent access control.
- The borrowed definition's aggregate arm catches the accumulation harm the frontier single-event floors are structurally blind to; neither species obliges anyone to act on what it sees.
- The third-party gate: contingency coverage keys on systems "deemed to be high-risk", and deeming requires seeing. Meaningful visibility beyond tier one runs 15-20 percent on named surveys (Deloitte CPO survey: 15 percent), while the document's own 2.12 concedes training data too large for humans to vet and issues difficult to attribute to any single source. Undeemed is uncovered.
- The 2am finding, stated fairly: this document was never going to be in the room at 2am; it is for the months before, when the room gets built. At 2am what holds is what was built, and this document, unlike the labs', obliges no one to have built anything. Its mid-incident register shows once directly: GV-1.3-006 suggests re-evaluating organisational risk tolerances in conditions including where "severe harms are actually occurring".

## Open questions

- Revision exposure runs on both texts: the core's revision is live and politically tasked (misinformation, DEI and climate eliminations; the profile carries the sixteen-occurrence Information Integrity footprint measured at the parent dive), while the profile's own promised future revisions have no commissioning authority behind them. Who revises an orphan?
- The unexecuted instance: no organisation has yet published the profile the core actually defines, with a named tolerance in it. The slot's function is vacant corpus-wide; third parties (the labs functionally, Berkeley's profiles formally) occupy it from outside.
- Alignment-washing: "consistent with NIST 600-1" trading as attestation in procurement and statute against a text with zero failable content -- footnote 13's coverage trap at ecosystem scale.
- An internal inconsistency stands in the published text: page 3 directs readers to risk mappings "in tables in Appendix B"; Appendix B contains only references.
- "Highly capable models" (GV-1.3-003) is used undefined, where the RSP defines the near-identical term as a load-bearing trigger: shared frontier vocabulary without the frontier machinery.
